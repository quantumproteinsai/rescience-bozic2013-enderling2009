\def \articleABSTRACT{Enderling et al. (2009) introduced a cellular automaton of cancer stem cells and their non-stem progeny and reported that increasing the spontaneous death rate of non-stem cells can paradoxically make tumours grow larger, by freeing space for stem cell divisions. We re-implemented the model from the compact specification of Poleszczuk and Enderling (2014). The paradox is reproduced: after 180 days, tumours grow from 56{,}922 cells at a death rate of 0 to 167{,}773 at 0.2, with 12-fold more stem cells, and about 71 \% of cells have no free neighbour, as reported. The published tumour size, about 140{,}000 cells after 180 days, is not reproduced: reading the migration rule in cell widths gives 60{,}692 cells, the literal reading 328{,}155. The size depends steeply on the migration probability and on when spontaneous death is tested, two rules the specification leaves open; the original code could not be found.}
